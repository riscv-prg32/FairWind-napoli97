# Game design

The campaign alternates between management and action. Its decision rhythm is inspired by modern motorsport-management games: a finite cash balance, sponsor retainers, win bonuses, race prizes, performance-gated sponsor tiers, and competing development priorities. Hull work raises efficiency and maximum speed; sails improve drive; crew improves response; strategy improves tactical performance. Five named races provide a compact season arc.

On the water the player is the helmsman and the mainsheet trimmer. The camera sits behind the stern, so the trim of the main, the set of a kite and the next mark all read from the same view. When a mark or a rival comes within 70 m the view switches overhead for close-quarters steering, and it switches back beyond 100 m.

The race is decided by the same things that decide real 12-Metre races:
- **Starts.** Timing the approach from the box, under the match-racing entry procedure.
- **Wind.** Reading shifts in an oscillating SW breeze: tack on headers and don't overstand the layline.
- **Trim.** Easing and trimming for the apparent wind.
- **Sail changes.** Choosing and timing spinnaker and gennaker hoists and drops, which cost time.
- **Traffic.** Handling boats under RRS 10–14, 18 and 31. A+B serves a penalty turn.

Yachts carry momentum, so every tack, gybe and luff has a price.

The field is larger than the screen and bounded by the Bay itself: Naples to leeward, Sorrento and Posillipo on either side, and a race-area limit to the SW. Three selectable course tables provide windward/run, Olympic triangle, and 1992 IACC Z racing.

All syndicates and sponsors are invented. Multiplayer uses one locally authoritative yacht and up to three remote PRG32 snapshot yachts. AI fills empty slots so every race retains a four-boat fleet.

Each venue uses a distinctive low-memory skyline: Castel dell'Ovo/Santa Lucia, Vesuvius, Capri/Faraglioni, Sorrento, and the Naples waterfront.
