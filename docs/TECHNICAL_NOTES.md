# Technical notes

The asset generator converts yacht artwork into four-bitplane storage ordered by frame, plane, row, and byte. Jib and spinnaker configurations are precomposed as clean top-down silhouettes, then recoloured through four syndicate-specific 16-entry RGB565 palettes covering hull, trim, and sails. Rotations are generated offline, so runtime drawing does not use trigonometry or heap allocation.

Each component is rendered at 128×128 before rotation and box-filtered downsampling. The generator then precomposes hull, mainsail, and either jib or spinnaker into two 32×32, four-bitplane frame banks. Runtime palette substitution gives Partenope, Libeccio, Atlantic Union, and Southern Cross distinct cyan, red, purple, or yellow hull-and-sail treatments without duplicating pixel planes.

The refined hull source interprets Il Moro di Venezia V's approximately 22.9 m length and 5.5 m beam as a readable 32×32 sprite rather than attempting a scale drawing. Its long, narrow 1992 IACC silhouette uses deep Venetian-red topsides, a brighter sheer stripe, pale deck inset, compact working stern, fine bow, multi-layer waterline, wake, cockpit, two crew positions, winches, rail lines, mast step, and bow fitting. Mainsails and jibs use pale cream/white cloth with restrained grey panels, seams, and battens. Both fore-and-aft sails now fall on the same leeward side: the jib occupies the bow triangle while the larger mainsail extends aft of the mast. Spinnakers use radial three-tone panels so team-palette substitution preserves four fictional syndicate identities. No real sponsor marks or exact Il Moro livery are reproduced. Dimensions, frame counts, and plane counts remain unchanged, preserving the portable cartridge budget.

Performance uses integer interpolation through ORC-rated 12‑Metre velocities at 8, 12, and 16 knots TWS. See `docs/POLAR_MODEL.md`. With no spinnaker, targets above 100° TWA are reduced to 72%; carrying it below 90° TWA reduces the target to 55%. AI hoists automatically from 110° TWA.

Five authored 320×48 panorama sources share a Mediterranean palette. Runtime versions retain 8-bit palette indices and are resized to 320×24, then compressed as row-safe `(run length, palette index)` byte pairs. The renderer expands horizontal runs directly, avoiding a framebuffer-sized decode buffer. The scenes depict Santa Lucia/Castel dell'Ovo, Vesuvius, Capri/Faraglioni, Sorrento, and the Naples waterfront/Castel Nuovo.

Close-quarters rendering switches automatically to a labelled overhead tactical grid whenever the player's yacht is within five yacht lengths (65 world pixels) of another yacht or any course/finish buoy. When every such object is farther than that threshold, the usual scenic panorama is restored immediately.

The camera is course-aligned instead of north-up: upscreen is the south-westerly windward end and downscreen is the north-easterly/leeward end. This matches the common daytime summer sea-breeze setup described for the Gulf of Naples, where SW and NE winds alternate over the daily cycle. The runtime starts within one 11.25° heading step of SW and periodically resamples only that narrow band. See Saviano et al., [Wind Direction Data from a Coastal HF Radar System in the Gulf of Naples](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs13071333), especially the seasonal overview and summer wind rose.

Audio declares eight instruments with explicit left/right pan pairs and three looped tracks. Every track starts voices 0–7. The packed block is generated by the firmware's `tools/prg32audio_pack.py`.

Race timing is expressed in simulated seconds. One simulated second advances every three update frames, so a 60 Hz target compresses the ten-minute pre-start to about 30 real seconds and the 30-minute race limit to about 90 real seconds. The pre-start implements the event's match-racing sailing instructions: warning/class signal at −10/−5, International Code P at −4, mandatory entry from the assigned port or starboard gate at −2, P removal at −1, and class-flag removal at zero. Tracks 3 and 4 provide stereo short and long horn signals before returning to the race score. A valid start requires the correct box entry and a subsequent pre-start-to-course-side crossing after zero. The committee boat, box, line, signal flags, and pin buoy remain visible until every local, remote, or AI yacht has registered a valid start.

The package targets the portable 64 KiB cartridge profile used by PRG32 main. The builder receives both `--portable` and `--multiplayer`; post-build checks reject any architecture variant over 65,536 bytes. `tools/prg32_cli_64.py` temporarily corrects the Python builder/uploader's stale 32 KiB fallback while preserving the normal CLI. The included host stub checks C syntax, but only a real `PRG32_ROOT` checkout can produce packaged firmware artifacts.

Multiplayer follows the firmware snapshot API: `init`, `available`, `join`, `tick`, `set_local_state`, `set_input`, `get_peer_count`, `get_peer`, and `leave`. The client clamps the relay peer count to three, maps peer indices to boats 1–3, and publishes a compact flag byte containing leg, team, spinnaker, start-crossing, readiness, and finish state. Course-specific `v7` room signatures prevent clients with different layouts, starting-line state, or prevailing-wind logic from joining. A distributed all-ready handshake starts two-to-four-player races without assigning a permanent host.

Course navigation is table driven. The windward/run, Olympic triangle, and 1992 IACC Z layouts contain three, five, and six ordered roundings respectively, fitting the three-bit multiplayer leg field. The AI and player validation read the same coordinate arrays. A mark advances only inside an 18-pixel capture box; after the final mark, the only valid completion is a southbound crossing of the finish segment between the committee boat and finish buoy. Rendering compares the local yacht's leg with the table index to pulse a gold frame around the next target. Multiplayer uses separate v7 room names for each selected course, preventing mismatched layouts from sharing a race.

The basic RRS engine becomes active at the preparatory signal. For every contact pair it first checks Rule 13 tacking status, then Rule 18 mark-room inside a 40-pixel three-length zone, Rule 10 tack, Rule 11 overlap/windward projection, and Rule 12 ahead/astern projection. Rule 14 contact response halves speed and separates hull centres. A sub-eight-pixel mark pass triggers Rule 31. The selected infringement is retained for the HUD until the player holds B through 32 heading steps; AI yachts serve the same 360-degree turn automatically. This deterministic ordering avoids contradictory simultaneous calls in the compact simulation.
